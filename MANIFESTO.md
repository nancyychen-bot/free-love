# Free Love — Manifesto

*A dating app that wants to lose you as a user.*

---

## The problem we exist to answer

Every major dating app shares one fatal conflict of interest: **their revenue depends on you staying single.**

A matched, happy couple is a churned customer. A lonely person who keeps paying is the ideal user. So the entire industry — quietly, rationally, and at enormous human cost — optimizes for *engagement* instead of *outcomes*. The longer you struggle, the more they earn.

Once you see that incentive, every dark pattern stops being a mystery and starts being a *strategy*:

- **The swipe is a slot machine.** Variable-ratio reinforcement — the most addictive reward schedule known — keeps your thumb moving. It was never designed to find you a person.
- **Scarcity is manufactured.** Limited likes, rationed visibility, "see who liked you" paywalls. The friction is invented so you can pay to remove it.
- **Attention is auctioned.** Boosts and super-likes sell visibility to the highest bidder, so everyone else's experience quietly degrades until they pay too.
- **You are secretly ranked.** Desirability scores sort people into invisible leagues and call it "compatibility."
- **Depth is designed out.** A photo-first interface trains you to judge a human being in 400 milliseconds.
- **Ghosting is a feature, not a bug.** Infinite alternatives mean no one is ever accountable to anyone.

We are not going to out-engineer these companies. We are going to **refuse to be them.**

---

## Our unfair advantage

Because Free Love is non-profit and open source, we can adopt the one metric the incumbents structurally cannot afford:

> **Success is someone deleting the app because they found their person.**

That single inversion frees us from every dark pattern at once. We don't need you addicted. We don't need you anxious. We don't need you paying to be seen. We need you *gone*, happy, and telling your friends why.

---

## The lines we will not cross

These are load-bearing. If a future feature violates one of these, the feature is wrong — not the principle.

1. **We optimize for exits, not engagement.** Our north-star metric is successful departures. We will measure them, publish them, and celebrate them. We will never measure "time in app" as a goal.

2. **No pay-to-win. Ever.** Nobody's visibility is auctioned. Nobody buys an advantage over anybody else. Costs are covered by transparent, optional, flat-rate donations or membership — never by selling advantage, attention, or anxiety.

3. **No selling data. No ad targeting. No surveillance.** We collect the minimum, we say exactly what we keep, and we never monetize who you are. This is an ethic *and* a moat — it is the one thing they cannot copy without dismantling their business.

4. **No secret ranking.** We will not sort people into desirability tiers. How matching works is open source and explainable. If we surface two people to each other, we can tell them why.

5. **No manufactured scarcity, no addictive loops.** No infinite deck. No variable-reward mechanics. No artificial limits designed to be paid away. Slowness and depth are features, not failures.

6. **Substance before (or beside) faces.** The product is built to reward who someone *is*, not how fast you can judge how they look.

7. **Accountability over disposability.** We design against ghosting and toward kind closure — the ability to end a conversation honestly instead of vanishing.

8. **Open source as trust.** The matching logic is not a black box that secretly grades you. Anyone can read how they are being matched. In this category, that is radical, and it is our credibility.

---

## What AI is allowed to do here

We will use modern tooling to be genuinely *smarter* and *deeper* than 2015-era matching — but under one rule:

> **AI serves depth and safety. Never engagement.**

The moment a feature's real goal is "more time in app," it has become the enemy, no matter how clever it is. AI may help people understand each other, open up, stay safe, and be matched on what they actually mean. It may not manipulate, fabricate intimacy, or manufacture compulsion.

## Who this is for

It's for people who are tired of being the product. It's for people who want depth and have been handed a casino. It's for us, honestly — Justin and Nancy — because we'd like to find our people too, and we don't trust anyone else to build the place we'd want to do it.

If we build this right, the best advertisement is a couple who met here, left here, and never looked back.

— **Free Love**
